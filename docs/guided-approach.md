# Guided approach with local obstacle avoidance

Use **Locate → Guided approach on the can phone**. The iPhone uses the existing relative Locate bearing/range, live LiDAR floor/obstacle samples, and ARKit heading/position for short detours. The Pi still executes the same simple authenticated commands. No Pi code changes, ML model, or server flags are needed.

## Preview first

1. Install the same updated app on both phones. Connect the can/player roles with camera-assisted bearing enabled. Preview can run with no Pi URL; disconnect motor power for a handheld test.
2. The measured mount is **25 cm from floor to middle camera**. Keep the rear camera forward and tilted down enough to see nearby ground. The mount initializes the floor reference, which detected floor surfaces confirm/refine. No calibration tap is required.
3. Close Lab and open **Locate → Preview guidance**. Select a 1/2/3 m stopping distance. In preview, no motion commands are generated by guidance.
4. The fan shows nine checked headings from 60° left to 60° right in 15° steps. Green means the path passes current observation checks; orange means an obstacle; dashed gray means insufficient observations. Cyan marks the selected heading. An extra ray checks the exact committed detour direction while turning. Left/right readouts summarize available lanes.
5. On an open path, it should point toward the player. Put a box in the direct path about 0.8–1 m away, leaving space on both sides. It should select an observed side and report, for example, **Avoid obstacle · turn left 45°**. The actual choice depends on measured geometry; no particular side or angle is guaranteed.
6. Carry/rotate the phone in the suggested direction. The remaining angle should decrease from measured ARKit heading, then the message should become **Detour forward**. Move along that short detour: it should eventually turn back toward the player's live UWB bearing. Keep the player stationary for initial tests.
7. Block both observed sides, lose tracking, or obscure depth: it must stop. If side lanes are gray, pause and inspect/scan manually with motor power disconnected. It never declares an unobserved side clear. If the first visible floor is too far away, tilt down; distant floor cannot fill the unseen near band.

## Motor test

1. Set the Pi URL/token 12345 on both phones, connect, and run the existing `python3 pi/server.py`. The bridge must be enabled and stopped. Release any previous Lab inspection hold first.
2. Verify wheel polarity and phone mounting with manual controls: phone forward/right must match chassis forward/right. Use a controlled arena with clearance around the whole can, a spotter, and the physical motor-power kill switch.
3. Start preview on the can phone. When it proposes a valid movement, tap **Start guided approach**. This explicitly enables motor commands; preview does not enable them automatically.
4. Output is 100% normal forward, detour forward and pivot in the driver's command scale, subject to the team-approved 150/255 duty cap. These are not measured speed or voltage. Validate deadband, traction and stopping distance on hardware.
5. A blocked/unknown chosen path, arrival, stale sensors or command acknowledgements, AR reset, fault/disconnection, Stop, leaving Locate, opening Lab, or app background ends the run. Recovery only changes preview advice; Start must be pressed again. Player manual/legacy Summon starts are disabled while guided driving is announced active; Player Stop remains available.

## Local planning rules

- The player target uses current can-side UWB distance/bearing directly; the cyan world-map marker is not used for steering. UWB must be connected, finite and less than 250 ms old. Arrival is selected phone-to-phone distance plus 15 cm.
- LiDAR/AR data must be less than 400 ms old, tracking Normal, and the floor reference reliable. Depth processing is 5 Hz and steering/lease renewal 10 Hz.
- Each heading checks a 46 cm observed-floor lane (30 cm bucket + 8 cm per side), starting 15 cm ahead and looking out to 1.4 m. Three of five columns per consecutive 25 cm row need floor observations. At least 90 cm of consecutively observed floor is required for a candidate.
- Known obstacles within 35 cm of a candidate centerline are rejected through a wider 70 cm obstacle check. Supported points within 35 cm of the camera ground position stop all movement, including pivoting. Floor coverage still uses the 46 cm core; these sampling heuristics do not certify the full swept footprint or outer margin as clear.
- A clear heading toward the player is preferred. Otherwise, candidates are scored by direction toward the player, amount of turn and observed clearance. Ties are deterministic. The chosen detour points 60 cm ahead in the shared AR world; that waypoint stays fixed as the phone turns.
- A committed detour retains its side. Every sensor update rechecks the exact heading toward its waypoint in addition to the fixed fan; blocked or unobserved routes stop instead of flipping sides. An active detour is limited to 8 s and stops if it moves more than 90 cm from the waypoint (possible wrong motor direction/tracking).
- Turn advice reports the remaining angle to the waypoint using ARKit heading/position, or the UWB angle for a direct approach. Pivot starts above about 13° and normally ends within about 6°; a small overshoot can proceed without reversing the pivot if the current forward lane is clear. A blocked straight lane forces the turn to finish even inside that tolerance. Direct player approach uses the checked fan heading nearest the UWB bearing. Positive bearing requests right; physical polarity must be checked.
- A detour completes within 18 cm of its waypoint, or can rejoin the player after at least 35 cm progress and three distinct depth frames showing a clear player-facing lane. Repeated control ticks on one depth frame do not count as extra confirmation.
- Pi movement acknowledgements must arrive within 250 ms. Malformed/non-confirming replies stop the run. Pi separately enforces its 300 ms command watchdog, ownership, sequence ordering and expiry. These limits are not measured total stopping latency.

## Floor and sensor limitations

The measured 25 cm mount seeds the world floor once on Normal tracking. Surface detection confirms/refines it through ARKit-classified floor planes or a broad horizontal depth-surface heuristic. Three consistent processed frames establish a measured candidate; an unconfirmed reference expires after 1.2 s. Camera movement does not drag the reference with it, and resets reacquire automatically. A large tabletop can still be ambiguous for the depth-only fallback: compare the displayed camera height with the actual lens height.

Green camera dots/map cells indicate observed floor within ±6 cm of the reference; gray camera dots mean no established floor. Red/orange/cyan camera dots indicate range, not object identity. Unobserved map cells stay dark.

There are still near/side/rear blind spots. Depth below 15 cm, low-confidence data, low obstacles below 8 cm and geometry above 1.5 m are omitted. The front camera cannot certify the swept area of a pivot, and the camera's ground position is only an approximate robot center. Wheel/chassis protrusions and mounting offsets need physical validation. Ultrasound is not integrated into this mode. Keep the arena clear around the can; this is an experimental local controller, not general room navigation or a guaranteed route around every obstacle. Narrow camera field of view can legitimately leave both detours unknown.

## Verification

Pure Swift tests cover candidate geometry, clearance inflation, left/right choice, unknown/enclosed paths, turn completion from measured heading, side commitment, distinct-frame rejoin, freshness, arrival, timeout, session reset and measured-floor behavior. A synthetic kinematic test exercises a box detour and return to the player. These do not validate real sensor accuracy or motor dynamics. Simulator checks cover offline navigation and disabled guided start without hardware. Driving still needs the controlled physical test above.


## Floor regression diagnostics

Lab runs the floor/map pipeline without the avoidance fan. Only Guided approach requests that fan, using a 4-pixel depth stride while floor detection/map keep their denser 2-pixel stride. Processing always takes the newest available AR frame, discards captures older than 150 ms before work, and timestamps snapshots at camera capture. Queue/processing delay now counts toward freshness; data already 400 ms old at publication is marked unavailable.

A supported current floor is preferred over unrelated tracked plane anchors. Anchor updates must be corroborated by broad horizontal depth samples near their height; if the old reference has no support, the detector can still recover to a genuinely measured surface. Lab now exposes floor-point percentage, depth-processing milliseconds and camera-frame age. Floor percentage is diagnostic only; it is not a minimum floor-to-object ratio for movement. Check these alongside floor source and measured camera height when debugging red/orange floor returns.

Moving the phone up/down should change the measured camera height while the same real floor keeps its classification. UWB direction convergence is separate from automatic floor initialization. A stale incorrect floor reference can now recover upward after expiry, rather than remaining locked until an AR reset.


## Guidance-to-Pi actuation and maximum output

Enter the Pi URL in Connect on both phones, run `python3 pi/server.py` on the Pi, and use Locate on the can phone. Start Preview guidance, then **Start guided approach** when the motor bridge is connected and stopped. This sends the chosen forward/left/right commands to authenticated `/control` with increasing sequences and renewals every 100 ms; stop/arrival/blocked guidance sends Stop. The live Pi motor-command readout shows requested left/right percentages, not encoder-measured motion.

All guided movement requests are 100%. Manual defaults migrate to 100% (the slider can deliberately lower it), and Legacy Summon no longer applies separate forward/pivot reductions. Driver `MAX_DUTY` is **150/255** for the confirmed 6 V motors / 12 V supply: 100% means this capped duty, not full 12 V PWM. Direct calls above 100% are clamped; Stop always disables enable pins. Do not raise the hardware duty cap above 150/255. Actual terminal voltage depends on supply, driver losses and load; this is not a regulated voltage setting.

Update the Python/driver files on Pi and restart its server as well as rebuilding both phone apps. First verify wheel signs and stopping with wheels lifted; the higher command output changes turn rate and stopping distance, so physical avoidance behavior must be checked again before a floor run.
