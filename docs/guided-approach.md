# Guided approach with local obstacle avoidance

Use **Locate → Guided approach on the player phone**. The mounted phone can stay on any tab except Lab; its guidance controls remain available as a fallback. The iPhone uses the existing relative Locate bearing/range, live LiDAR floor/obstacle samples, and ARKit heading/position for short detours. The Pi still executes the same simple authenticated commands. No Pi code changes, ML model, or server flags are needed.

## Preview first

1. Install the same updated app on both phones. Connect the can/player roles with camera-assisted bearing enabled. Preview can run with no Pi URL; disconnect motor power for a handheld test.
2. The measured mount is **25 cm from floor to middle camera**. Keep the rear camera forward and tilted down enough to see nearby ground. The mount initializes the floor reference, which detected floor surfaces confirm/refine. No calibration tap is required.
3. Close Lab on the can and open **Locate → Preview guidance on the player**. Select a 1/2/3 m stopping distance. In preview, no motion commands are generated by guidance.
4. The player receives live advice, remaining turn, floor status, pause/stop reason and Pi output. The detailed fan remains on the can phone. The fan shows nine checked headings from 60° left to 60° right in 15° steps. Green means the path passes current observation checks; orange means an obstacle; dashed gray means insufficient observations. Cyan marks the selected heading. An extra ray checks the exact committed detour direction while turning. Left/right readouts summarize available lanes.
5. On an open path, it should point toward the player. Put a box in the direct path about 0.8–1 m away, leaving space on both sides. It should select an observed side and report, for example, **Avoid obstacle · turn left 45°**. The actual choice depends on measured geometry; no particular side or angle is guaranteed.
6. Carry/rotate the phone in the suggested direction. The remaining angle should decrease from measured ARKit heading, then the message should become **Detour forward**. Move along that short detour: it should eventually turn back toward the player's live UWB bearing. Keep the player stationary for initial tests.
7. Block both observed sides, lose tracking, or obscure depth: it must stop. If side lanes are gray, pause and inspect/scan manually with motor power disconnected. It never declares an unobserved side clear. If the first visible floor is too far away, tilt down; distant floor cannot fill the unseen near band.

## Motor test

1. Set the Pi URL/token 12345 on both phones, connect, and run the existing `python3 pi/server.py`. The bridge must be enabled and stopped. Release any previous Lab inspection hold first.
2. Verify wheel polarity and phone mounting with manual controls: phone forward/right must match chassis forward/right. Use a controlled arena with clearance around the whole can, a spotter, and the physical motor-power kill switch.
3. Start preview on the player phone. When it proposes a valid movement, tap **Start guided approach**. This explicitly enables motor commands; preview does not enable them automatically.
4. All forward, reverse and pivot movement requests are 100%. The manual speed slider and guided power taper are removed. All output remains subject to the 170/255 duty cap and motor trim. These are not measured speed or voltage. Validate deadband, traction and stopping distance on hardware.
5. Arrival, command failure, AR reset, fault/disconnection, Stop, opening Lab, or app background ends the run. A blocked/unobserved route pauses stopped for up to 8 s; fresh clear measurements can resume after three stable frames. A measured obstacle within 5 cm triggers an explicit emergency stop. A supported front obstacle within 35 cm initiates bounded backup recovery. Missing UWB measurements stop the motors and wait up to 8 s; missing LiDAR/AR/clearance-scan measurements wait up to 2 s. Both show PAUSED. Three distinct safe depth frames and UWB updates captured after the pause, plus a Pi-confirmed stopped state, can resume within the bounded pause. Longer gaps require Start again. Tab changes alone do not end guidance. Player manual/legacy Summon starts are disabled while guided driving is announced active; Player Stop remains available.

## Local planning rules

- The player target uses current can-side UWB distance/bearing directly; the cyan world-map marker is not used for steering. UWB must be connected, finite and less than 250 ms old. Arrival is selected phone-to-phone distance plus 15 cm.
- LiDAR/AR data must be less than 400 ms old, tracking Normal, and the floor reference reliable. Depth processing is 5 Hz and steering/lease renewal 10 Hz.
- Each heading checks a 46 cm observed-floor lane (30 cm bucket + 8 cm per side), starting 15 cm ahead and looking out to 1.4 m. Three of five columns per consecutive 25 cm row need floor observations. At least 35 cm of consecutively observed floor is required for a candidate (the 25 cm sampling rows make the first complete row end at 40 cm).
- Only raised samples within 35 cm horizontal distance from the camera affect lane blocking. Obstacles use the same 46 cm corridor as floor observations; the former wider 70 cm obstacle check is removed. Supported points within 5 cm of the camera ground position trigger the emergency stop. Floor coverage still uses the 46 cm core; these sampling heuristics do not certify the full swept footprint or outer margin as clear.
- A clear heading toward the player is preferred. Otherwise, candidates are scored by direction toward the player, amount of turn and observed clearance. Ties are deterministic. The chosen detour points 60 cm ahead in the shared AR world; that waypoint stays fixed as the phone turns.
- A committed detour retains its side. Every sensor update rechecks the exact heading toward its waypoint in addition to the fixed fan; a newly observed obstacle can trigger replanning on the same side, while unobserved routes wait stopped instead of flipping sides. An active detour is limited to 8 s and stops if it moves more than 90 cm from the waypoint (possible wrong motor direction/tracking).
- Turn advice reports the remaining angle to the waypoint using ARKit heading/position, or the UWB angle for a direct approach. Direct pursuit starts a pivot above about 13° near the selected stopping range, increasing linearly to 20° with 2 m remaining beyond the arrival threshold. A pursuit pivot normally ends at 75% of that threshold, or earlier according to rotation feedback. Detour pivots retain the approximately 13° start / 6° finish thresholds; a small overshoot can proceed without reversing the pivot if the current forward lane is clear. A blocked straight lane forces the turn to finish even inside that tolerance. Heading feedback changes direction and can release a pivot earlier when rotation predicts alignment; it does not reduce requested motor power. Direct approach steers using the exact measured UWB bearing; the nearest fan lane certifies clearance, avoiding 15° steering quantization. Positive bearing requests right; physical polarity must be checked.
- A detour completes within 18 cm of its waypoint, or can rejoin the player after at least 35 cm progress and three distinct depth frames showing a clear player-facing lane. Repeated control ticks on one depth frame do not count as extra confirmation.
- A movement reply older than 250 ms cannot confirm movement. In-flight steering requests finish before issuing a changed command; only Stop cancels them. The local missing-ack circuit breaker ends the run at 600 ms, while the unchanged Pi watchdog already stops motors after 300 ms without accepted renewals. Malformed/non-confirming replies stop the run. Pi separately enforces its 300 ms command watchdog, ownership, sequence ordering and expiry. These limits are not measured total stopping latency.

## Floor and sensor limitations

The measured 25 cm mount seeds the world floor once on Normal tracking. Surface detection confirms/refines it through ARKit-classified floor planes or a broad horizontal depth-surface heuristic. Three consistent processed frames establish a measured candidate; an unconfirmed reference expires after 1.2 s. Camera movement does not drag the reference with it, and resets reacquire automatically. A large tabletop can still be ambiguous for the depth-only fallback: compare the displayed camera height with the actual lens height.

Green camera dots/map cells indicate observed floor within ±6 cm of the reference; gray camera dots mean no established floor. Red/orange/cyan camera dots indicate range, not object identity. Unobserved map cells stay dark.

There are still near/side/rear blind spots. Depth below 15 cm, low-confidence data, low obstacles below 8 cm and geometry above 1.5 m are omitted. The front camera cannot certify the swept area of a pivot, and the camera's ground position is only an approximate robot center. Wheel/chassis protrusions and mounting offsets need physical validation. Ultrasound is not integrated into this mode. Keep the arena clear around the can; this is an experimental local controller, not general room navigation or a guaranteed route around every obstacle. Narrow camera field of view can legitimately leave both detours unknown.

## Verification

Pure Swift tests cover candidate geometry, clearance inflation, left/right choice, unknown/enclosed paths, turn completion from measured heading, side commitment, distinct-frame rejoin, freshness, arrival, timeout, session reset and measured-floor behavior. A synthetic kinematic test exercises a box detour and return to the player. These do not validate real sensor accuracy or motor dynamics. Simulator checks cover offline navigation and disabled guided start without hardware. Driving still needs the controlled physical test above.


## Floor regression diagnostics

Lab runs the floor/map pipeline without the avoidance fan. Only Guided approach requests that fan, using a 4-pixel depth stride while floor detection/map keep their denser 2-pixel stride. Processing always takes the newest available AR frame, discards captures older than 150 ms before work, and timestamps snapshots at camera capture. Queue/processing delay now counts toward freshness; data already 400 ms old at publication is marked unavailable.

A supported current floor is preferred over unrelated tracked plane anchors. Anchor updates must be corroborated by broad horizontal depth samples near their height; if the old reference has no support, the detector can still recover to a genuinely measured surface. Lab now exposes floor-point percentage, depth-processing milliseconds and camera-frame age. Floor percentage is diagnostic only; it is not a minimum floor-to-object ratio for movement. Check these alongside floor source and measured camera height when debugging red/orange floor returns.

Moving the phone up/down should change the measured camera height while the same real floor keeps its classification. UWB direction convergence is separate from automatic floor initialization. A stale incorrect floor reference can now recover upward after expiry, rather than remaining locked until an AR reset.


## Guidance-to-Pi actuation and maximum output

Enter the Pi URL in Connect on both phones, run `python3 pi/server.py` on the Pi, and use Locate on the player phone. Start Preview guidance, then **Start guided approach** when the motor bridge is connected and stopped. This sends the chosen forward/left/right commands to authenticated `/control` with increasing sequences and renewals every 100 ms; stop/arrival/blocked guidance sends Stop. Blocked guidance may remain armed during its bounded wait. The live Pi motor-command readout shows requested left/right percentages, not encoder-measured motion.

All guided movement requests are 100%. Manual defaults migrate to 100% (the slider can deliberately lower it), and Legacy Summon no longer applies separate forward/pivot reductions. Driver `MAX_DUTY` is **170/255** for the confirmed 6 V motors / 12 V supply: 100% means this capped duty, not full 12 V PWM. Direct calls above 100% are clamped; Stop always disables enable pins. The user selected this hardware duty cap for testing. Actual terminal voltage depends on supply, driver losses and load; this is not a regulated voltage setting.

Update the Python/driver files on Pi and restart its server as well as rebuilding both phone apps. First verify wheel signs and stopping with wheels lifted; the higher command output changes turn rate and stopping distance, so physical avoidance behavior must be checked again before a floor run.

## Remote control and intermittent stops

Both phones need this app version. The player sends bounded-age, uniquely identified preview/start/stop requests; the can acknowledges requests and publishes guidance status at about 5 Hz. Target selection belongs to the player until an active run reports its selected target. A stale status disables Start, and unacknowledged requests show a timeout. Stop remains available and also uses the direct Pi endpoint when configured on the player. Guidance needs the Pi URL on the **can phone**; a player Pi URL is useful for manual driving and a second Stop path.

A player-started run requires a player heartbeat every 200 ms. Missing heartbeats for 800 ms end the run, even if MPC has not yet reported a disconnect. Reconnecting never starts movement automatically. Keep both apps foregrounded. Can-side camera processing and navigation continue when changing between Play/Locate/Connect; Lab and background still stop guidance.

Previous steering changes canceled HTTP requests before acknowledgements arrived, and every single stale sensor frame latched a stop. Requests now serialize, old canceled callbacks cannot clear a newer Stop, and bounded sensor and route waits can recover automatically. Measured obstacles within 5 cm, motor faults, command rejection, watchdog timeout, AR reset and arrival remain final stops. Missing/blocked routes now get an 8 s stopped wait, with one bounded, rear-unchecked backup when a front obstacle is actually observed. Motor limits remain at most 100% commands scaled to 170/255 PWM.

Run `swiftc ios/TrashBotUWB/DepthGeometry.swift ios/TrashBotUWB/GameState.swift tests/guidance/main.swift -o /tmp/trashketball-guidance-tests` and then that binary for protocol and recovery checks. Hardware validation: start preview/start from the player with the can on Play; verify visible turn advice, observe a brief sensor-unavailable pause and stable recovery; a LiDAR/AR gap over 2 s or UWB gap over 8 s must require explicit Start. Do not place a hand close to moving wheels or rely on the 5 cm gate for contact protection. Check Stop and background/disconnect separately. Test steering with the player stationary and inspect the now-continuous remaining angle; PID tuning needs measured turn response on the actual chassis.

## Turning, drift and blocked-route recovery

Update/restart the Pi server **and rebuild both phone apps** for these changes. Guidance uses the same full-power `forward`/`left`/`right` command path as manual controls. The Pi still supports differential `drive` packets for compatibility, but guidance no longer lowers wheel percentages. All sensing and navigation run on the can phone; Python only validates and applies wheel requests, gains and the watchdog.

All guided and manual movement requests use 100%, including pivots close to alignment. Existing heading deadbands and early turn release affect turn duration, not power. Forward PD wheel-speed reduction is disabled to honor fixed full-power requests. Full-power turning still needs chassis testing for overshoot.

For observed **rightward drift**, `LEFT_MOTOR_GAIN = 0.90`, `RIGHT_MOTOR_GAIN = 1.00` in `pi/server.py` reduce the faster left wheel. This is a starting trim, not an encoder calibration. Gains apply to manual and guided commands, including reverse and pivots. Adjust the left gain toward 1.00 if it begins drifting left. Do not increase either gain above 1.00 or raise `MAX_DUTY`. The live Pi L/R values now include this trim before wiring inversion.

A route can be unavailable because of measured obstacles **or missing observations**. Guidance holds both motors stopped, displays which case it sees, and rechecks for up to 8 s. Three distinct safe depth frames and fresh UWB updates after the hold, plus a confirmed Pi Stop, permit resuming. A subsequent LiDAR/AR failure shortens the hold to at most 2 s from its original start. A very close obstacle, tracking-world reset, motor/connection failure or expired wait still requires explicit Start. A committed detour may hit its separate 8 s timeout sooner.

The ultrasonic sensor faces forward and is not used by this controller; there is no rear clearance measurement. Automatic backup is now enabled but strictly bounded to approximately 15 cm of AR-measured travel or 0.6 s from command dispatch, whichever ends first; an observed clear side after at least 5 cm travel can end it sooner. It does not repeat blindly when no side opens. Keep a spotter behind the robot and rear space clear. A rear distance sensor is still needed for protected reversing.

Hardware check: with wheels raised, verify manual forward now requests roughly L90/R100 at the 100% setting, then verify wheel polarity, Stop and timeout. In an open arena, keep the player stationary: all movement should use 100% requested power, and turns should end when heading reaches the release threshold. Recheck whether straight travel still drifts; the 10% fixed trim needs tuning against real floor/caster friction. Blocking the route should hold the can stationary, then recover only after stable visible clearance; a known immediate front obstacle should cause one bounded reverse, a stopped side check, a clear detour and then pursuit of the player.

## Recovering tracking without disconnecting everything

A missing horizontal bearing in an otherwise live UWB update leaves the phone link and shared camera/AR session intact. Motors pause for up to 8 s on stale/missing heading, then need Start if it has not recovered; the app does not substitute zero or keep driving on an old angle. Apple can report direction unavailable while distance remains available. Initialization/convergence messages are displayed separately from link status; move the phone only when the reported convergence reason requires it.

A Nearby Interaction timeout reruns the existing configuration on the same session. A suspended session resumes after the system's suspension-ended callback. Peer-ended ranging requests a fresh discovery token over the existing phone link. Duplicate tokens no longer unnecessarily rerun an already configured session. Recoverable invalidations create a fresh NI session with 0.5/1/2 s retries while keeping the external AR session; denied permission, invalid configuration and exhausted retries show an explicit Retry tracking fallback.

A dropped MPC phone link stops motion but keeps the app/Pi connection and shared camera session running, then restarts advertiser/browser discovery automatically. One invitation can be in flight at a time. Discovery errors retry without tearing down AR. Reconnection never restarts motors; press Start once fresh heading and clearance are ready. Explicit Disconnect cancels pending recovery work. On returning from a backgrounded app, the previously running connection starts again automatically, with motors stopped; a genuine camera/session interruption may need convergence again. Both apps should remain foregrounded during the game.

Apple references: [NI timeout/removal recovery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/nearbyinteraction/nisessiondelegate/session(_:didremove:reason:)), [resuming suspension](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/nearbyinteraction/nisessiondelegate/sessionsuspensionended(_:)), and [permission denial](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/nearbyinteraction/nierror/userdidnotallow).

## Simple obstacle recovery: back up, pick a side, pursue again

A separate supported-point front check looks inside the bucket-width corridor. It distinguishes an actual object immediately ahead from nearby side objects or missing floor. A front obstacle within 35 cm starts **one** automatic reverse burst. Planning/preview does not start its timer: limits begin when the reverse command is dispatched. Reverse remains a 100% request, with the existing Pi trim/PWM ceiling.

The burst ends after roughly 15 cm of AR displacement, 0.6 s of monotonic time, or at least 5 cm travel with a clear side already observed. The robot sends Stop and waits for confirmed zero motor output. It then chooses an observed clear left/right lane; normal three-new-frame confirmation prevents a single noisy scan from restarting a turn. After the short detour, the robot pursues the player's live UWB heading again. Another backup is allowed only after completing that detour with the front no longer blocked; if both sides stay unknown/blocked, it waits stopped instead of backing repeatedly. An expired wait still ends the run.

The old global 35 cm stop is now **5 cm measured horizontal distance from the camera ground position**, not calibrated bumper clearance. Emergency stops include the measured distance and the 5 cm limit in a persistent red message on the player phone. The depth pipeline still filters axial depth below 15 cm, and camera coverage/offsets introduce blind spots: this is **not reliable 5 cm collision protection**. No sensor/Pi watchdog, clock validation or explicit Stop check is bypassed.

Verification covers forward-hit support, side-only objects, backup distance/time limits, preview-versus-dispatch timing, sensor interruption during reverse, stopping before selecting a side, no repeated unchecked backups, return to the player heading, persistent emergency messages and reverse HTTP-to-mock-GPIO actuation. Physical rear clearance, momentum and stopping distance still require a supervised arena test.

Short-range tuning prioritizes fewer early corrections. The synthetic navigation test checks the 15 cm radius of the 30 cm-wide body against obstacle points; it does not guarantee the prior 8 cm swept margin, physical stopping distance, or collision-free hardware behavior. Rebuild the mounted-phone iOS app for these changes.
